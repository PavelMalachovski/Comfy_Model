# AI-persona brand and funnel playbook, audience evidence, KPIs, and the late-2026 legal, payment and reputation landscape for an adult-adjacent synthetic persona

*Compiled 2026-11-21 from English-language sources. Context: a photoreal AI-generated female persona (40-year-old yoga and pilates trainer "living in Dubai"); Fanvue is the paid hub; Instagram and Threads are SFW traffic; X and Telegram carry teasers.*

*Source labels: **[primary]** = regulator, statute or the platform's own page; **[law firm]**; **[press]**; **[academic]**; **[vendor]** = a company selling tools or services to creators, so expect marketing bias; **[anecdotal]** = practitioner or blog claim; **[unverified]** = seen only in a search snippet or behind a paywall, or sources conflict. Anything dated before 2025 is flagged "(pre-2025, may be outdated)". Risk ratings in the tables are my own judgement, built from the cited rules.*

## 1. Funnel architecture used by successful AI-persona operators and agencies

### Takeaway
The standard 2024–2026 funnel for AI personas runs in three stages: SFW photo and short-video **discovery** on Instagram (plus Threads and TikTok where rules allow), then **trust-building** layers (Telegram or X teasers, DMs, voice), then a **paid hub** on Fanvue. Fanvue is now the only major subscription platform that accepts photoreal AI personas built from scratch, and it pushes AI-assisted chat and voice. The "is she real?" ambiguity tactic has been penalised on Instagram since 31 Aug 2026: AI-person profiles without a label lose eligibility for recommendations. A funnel that openly discloses AI is now the only low-risk setup.

### Cited Findings

#### Platform roles and the basic funnel
- Business Insider (Nov 2024) describes Instagram as the top of the funnel. AI "women" accounts with big followings and brand deals put a Fanvue link in their bio ($7/month in the example), and the paysite holds more AI images and video. In BI's words, "Instagram and other social-media sites were just at the top of the conversion funnel." Courses teaching this model sell for about $220 — [Business Insider via AOL, 25 Nov 2024](https://www.aol.com/influencers-using-ai-women-lead-093001152.html) (pre-2025, may be outdated).
- 404 Media reviewed more than 1,000 AI-generated Instagram influencer accounts. 100 of them used deepfake content: real models' or adult performers' videos with the AI face swapped in, used to promote Fanvue- or OnlyFans-style subscriptions. Of those 100, 60 said they were AI in the bio and 40 did not. Meta deleted high-profile accounts after 404 Media contacted it, and Fanvue temporarily removed one account while it investigated — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ai-influencers-are-deepfaking-their-faces-onto-real-womens-bodies/); ["AI pimping" industry, 404 Media, Nov 2024](https://www.404media.co/inside-the-booming-ai-pimping-industry-3/); [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/episode/ai-pimping-accounts-are-exploding-on-social-media) (pre-2025, but this is the reputational backdrop of the whole category).
- **Instagram, 31 Aug 2026:** the "AI creator" label was renamed **"AI-generated profile."** Instagram "will limit the reach" of profiles that feature an AI-generated person without the label, and they "may become non-recommendable." Creators who add the label proactively see no change in reach. Status shows in Account Status and can be appealed. The profile label appears on the profile and alongside content unless the media already carries an AI label. Creators who only use AI tools in their process don't need it — [Instagram Creators blog, 31 Aug 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label) [primary]; coverage in [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/) and [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2246914/instagram-will-demote-ai-generated-influencers-if-they-dont-clearly-label-their-account/).
- Meta separately requires a per-post disclosure for photorealistic video or realistic audio that was generated or altered digitally. It applies "AI info" / "Made with AI" labels when it detects AI content that wasn't disclosed — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/761121959519495) [primary]; [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagrams-new-ai-label/).
- The Instagram announcement does not mention Threads or Facebook — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label).

#### Collaborations and SFS networks
- **SFS** ("shoutout for shoutout") is free mutual promotion between creators. Partners are found mostly in Telegram groups, then on X and Discord. Paid SFS services exist, e.g. $75/week for up to 350 shoutouts a day — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/onlyfans-glossary/onlyfans-sfs-shoutout-for-shoutout); [CPA.live](https://cpa.live/en/articles-en/what-is-sfs) [vendor/anecdotal].
- Human creators complain that AI face-swap accounts compete with them and cut their reach. One creator's reels fell from typical 1–5M views — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ai-influencers-are-deepfaking-their-faces-onto-real-womens-bodies/) (pre-2025). So human creators may resist SFS requests from AI accounts.

### Inferences
- **Recommended architecture for this client:**
  - *Instagram Reels and Threads* for discovery: SFW yoga, pilates and flexibility clips, plus age-positive "40 and strong" storytelling and Dubai-life scenes in the fixed home sets.
  - *Telegram* for community and trust: a free channel with polls, behind-the-scenes posts and labelled voice notes.
  - *X* for teasers flagged as adult content.
  - *Fanvue* for monetisation: subscriptions, PPV, and AI chat and voice that are disclosed as AI.

  This is the funnel BI and 404 Media describe, but run with disclosure instead of ambiguity.
- **Label the Instagram profile as an "AI-generated profile" now.** An unlabelled persona risks losing recommendations, which is fatal for a discovery channel. It also invites third-party "exposure," which research shows is the worst outcome for trust (see Q2).
- **Lore:** a life narrative that stays consistent (age, profession, the same rooms, recurring "friends" who may be other labelled AI personas) feeds parasocial engagement. Avoid fake appearances at real events or venues. The Wimbledon case shows these trigger "catfish" backlash and mockery, even when reach goes up.
- **Video:** short vertical video is the main discovery format on Reels and TikTok. Test Kling 3.0 and Veo 3.1 for identity consistency, and always tick Meta's AI-info disclosure on photoreal video.
- **Tactic-by-tactic compliance and risk:**

| Tactic | Platform-rule status | Ban risk | Reputation risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| SFW fitness Reels and Threads posts with the "AI-generated profile" label and per-post AI info on video | Compliant (IG Aug 2026 rule; Meta AI-info rule) | Low | Low–medium (some AI-skeptic audiences) |
| Unlabelled "is she real?" ambiguity on IG | Breaches the IG rule since 31 Aug 2026; profile can become non-recommendable | Medium (reach loss) | High (exposure backlash; worst-case trust effect) |
| Fanvue link in IG/Threads bio | Not covered by the IG label post; Meta's sexual-solicitation rules not checked here | Medium | Low–medium |
| Adult-flagged teasers on X, no nudity in avatar or header | Compliant with X's adult content policy | Low–medium | Medium |
| Telegram free channel plus Stars paid posts | Allowed; creator rewards "may be unavailable" for some topics; no illegal porn in public channels | Medium | Low–medium |
| AI DMs and voice notes on Fanvue, disclosed as AI | Matches the Fanvue disclosure norm; fits EU Art. 50(1) and NY/CA companion laws (Q5) | Low | Low |
| Undisclosed AI or human "chatters" posing as the persona "live" | Same conduct behind the OnlyFans class actions; conflicts with Art. 50(1) and NY/CA laws | Medium | High |
| Face-swapping the persona onto real creators' videos | Breaks Fanvue's real-person rule; Meta deletes such accounts; possible UK s.138 / TAKE IT DOWN exposure if intimate | High | Very high |
| Cross-promotion with other labelled AI personas ("sister"/friend lore) | Allowed if labelled | Low | Low |
| SFS with human creators via Telegram groups | Platform-neutral; partners may refuse AI | Low | Medium |
| Buying followers, likes or "subscribers" | Breaks platform terms and the FTC fake-indicator ban (Q5) | High | High |

## 2. Audience response to AI personas: disclosure, "is she real," parasocial dynamics, churn

### Takeaway
Experiments show a real **trust penalty from AI disclosure**, and it is largest when a third party exposes the AI use. Consumer enthusiasm for AI creator content fell sharply from 2023 to 2025. Even so, parasocial responses to virtual influencers can match those to humans, and paying audiences clearly exist (Fanvue's growth). The practical answer is proactive, consistent self-disclosure rather than ambiguity. Revenue and retention depend on interaction (messages, PPV), and the biggest backlash and legal risk is pretending a "live" person is chatting.

### Cited Findings

#### Disclosure and trust
- **Schilke & Reimann (2025, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, vol. 188).** Across 13 experiments with more than 5,000 participants, disclosing AI use lowered trust through reduced perceived legitimacy. Softer framings ("only for proofreading," "a human reviewed it") did not prevent the drop. Being exposed by a third party was the worst outcome. The penalty was smaller, but did not disappear, among people who view technology favourably — [University of Arizona](https://news.arizona.edu/news/disclosing-ai-use-can-backfire-research-shows); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v188y2025ics0749597825000172.html) [academic].
- ***Journal of Interactive Advertising* (2025), "Disclaimer! This Content Is AI-Generated."** AI disclosures raised AI knowledge and persuasion knowledge, which lowered trust in the ad and the organisation. A positive indirect path also exists, depending on which type of persuasion knowledge is triggered — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15252019.2025.2554149) [academic; abstract level].
- **Billion Dollar Boy, "Muse Two"** (fieldwork 20 Jun–1 Jul 2025; 4,000 US/UK consumers aged 16+, 1,000 creators, 1,000 marketers; released Oct 2025):
  - only 26% of consumers prefer AI-generated creator content to traditional creator content, down from 60% in 2023;
  - 32% see generative AI as a negative force in the creator economy, up from 18% in 2023;
  - only 45% feel confident spotting AI-generated creator content;
  - 60% of creators admit their AI-assisted content broke industry rules, often through mislabelling;
  - 79% of marketers are increasing spend on AI creator content.
  - Sources: [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/exclusive--ai-slop-threat-creator-economy); [Billion Dollar Boy](https://www.billiondollarboy.com/news/new-research-real-impact-ai-creator-economy); [Agility PR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/new-billion-dollar-boy-research-global-ad-spend-surges-on-ai-creator-content-despite-growing-consumer-resistance-to-ai-slop/).

#### "Is she real?" ambiguity and catfishing backlash
- **Wimbledon 2025:** an AI persona's courtside posts fooled many tennis fans even though her bio said she was AI. When it came out, the backlash made her go viral and widened her reach. A cricket star was mocked for having interacted with her as if she were real — [Tennis.com](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/ai-influencer-mia-zelu-went-viral-wimbledon-artificial-intelligence-social-media); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/wimbledon-it-girl-mia-zelu-isnt-real-ai-influencer-fools-150k-fans-sports-star-fake-1738138) [press]. Commentators also criticised AI influencers for promoting unrealistic beauty standards — [NationalWorld](https://www.nationalworld.com/culture/celebrity/ai-influencers-mia-zelu-alba-renai-5222935).
- Comments on an obviously AI account "read like a lot of people thought she's human" — [BI via AOL](https://www.aol.com/influencers-using-ai-women-lead-093001152.html) (pre-2025).
- **BBC investigation (2026):** about 60 accounts, mostly on Instagram, used **unlabelled** AI avatars (sexualised caricatures of Black women and real women's bodies) with chains of links to paid explicit content. TikTok banned about 20 accounts, citing "zero tolerance for content which promotes off-platform sexual services" — [Atlanta Black Star, 2 May 2026](https://atlantablackstar.com/2026/05/02/investigation-exposes-ai-scammers-hijacking-real-womens-bodies-and-creating-black-caricatures-to-peddle-explicit-content-targeting-white-men/); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/tiktok-bans-ai-impersonation-accounts-targeting-black-female-influencers/story) [secondary press].
- 404 Media found AI "influencers with Down syndrome" being used to sell nudes, part of the category's reputational baggage — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/people-are-using-ai-to-create-influencers-with-down-syndrome-who-sell-nudes/).
- Instagram's stated reason for its label: people "want to know when a profile features an AI-generated person" — [Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label).

#### Parasocial dynamics
- **Stein et al. (2022, *New Media & Society*; preregistered, N=179):** parasocial responses to a virtual and a human influencer did not differ significantly. The virtual influencer had a direct advantage, but it was cancelled out because viewers saw it as less "mentally human" and less similar to themselves — [UvA repository](https://dare.uva.nl/id/33d13b9c-3f1c-4b77-b17e-6daae1dcb008) (pre-2025).
- **2024 *Journal of Business Research* study (826 Instagram users):** human-like traits of virtual influencers shape the strength of parasocial bonds, through trustworthiness and wishful identification. Existing and prospective followers judge on different criteria — [University of Florida](https://www.jou.ufl.edu/2024/10/23/study-human-qualities-of-virtual-influencers-can-affect-trustworthiness-in-parasocial-relationships/) [academic].
- **2024 FIU dissertation:** credibility and authenticity matter for followers of both human and non-human influencers. Parasocial relationship and wishful identification weigh more for human influencers. Trust in sponsored posts mediated outcomes for all groups — [FIU](https://business.fiu.edu/academics/graduate/doctor-of-business-administration/docs/2024/maria-lovera-etd.pdf) [academic, dissertation].

#### Retention drivers and the "intimacy" problem
- OnlyFans has shifted toward one-off purchases. About 60% of creator revenue came from single purchases, and single-purchase revenue grew 70% in 2021–2023 against 9% for subscriptions — [Tubefilter, Sep 2024](https://tubefilter.com/2024/09/12/onlyfans-digital-products-revenue) (pre-2025).
- **Deceptive DMs:**
  - *N.Z. v. Fenix International* (filed July 2024; trial set for 2027) and a second proposed class action in 2025 allege that OnlyFans let agency "chatters" impersonate creators, including when sexting.
  - A Reuters investigation found 15 chatters who said they were told to keep up the fiction that they were the creators.
  - Sources: [ClassAction.org](https://classaction.org/news/onlyfans-lawsuit-alleges-subscribers-unknowingly-talk-with-paid-chatters-not-actual-content-creators); [404 Media](https://404media.co/onlyfans-sued-after-two-guys-realized-they-might-not-actually-be-talking-to-models); [Reuters via Philstar](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/); [Hagens Berman](https://hbsslaw.com/cases/onlyfans-chatters); [Lawyer Monthly, Apr 2025](https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2025/04/emotional-fraud-and-fake-dms-the-class-action-challenging-onlyfans-illusion-of-intimacy/).

### Inferences
- **Disclosure is now effectively decided by the rules** (Instagram label, EU Art. 50, Fanvue norms). The evidence predicts some loss of trust and engagement at the top of the funnel, but disclosing avoids the worst case: being exposed by others. Fanvue buyers have already chosen AI content, so disclosure probably costs less at the paid stage than on Instagram.
- **Make "AI" part of the brand story, not fine print.** Use a consistent "AI-generated" bio line and media labels, and openly tell the "AI trainer" story. This disarms the catfish narrative that drove the Wimbledon and BBC backlashes.
- **Parasocial levers:** perceived similarity and human-likeness strengthen bonds. A relatable 40-year-old, age-positive trainer narrative should raise perceived similarity for a mature audience. Consistent voice and lore help; deception does not.
- **Retention levers:** personal interaction (messages, voice, PPV) drives revenue. It must be clear who or what is replying. "Live" impersonation is the exact pattern being litigated against OnlyFans.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found on how AI disclosure affects **paid conversion** in adult subscription settings, or comparing churn for AI and human creators on Fanvue.
- A counter-review reporting no consistent disclosure penalty in news contexts was mentioned in search summaries but not verified.
- No data on how many paying fans know, or care, that a persona is AI.

## 3. Additional traffic sources not yet used (Reddit, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Pinterest, Bluesky, dating apps)

### Takeaway
No new channel offers both high reach and low risk for an AI persona linked to adult content.
- **Pinterest** and **YouTube Shorts** can carry SFW brand awareness without direct paysite links.
- **TikTok** works as discovery only if it is fully separated from the adult funnel; it has "zero tolerance" for promoting off-platform sexual services.
- **Reddit** has the best adult-conversion potential, but subreddit rules and anti-spam enforcement govern it.
- **Bluesky** allows labelled adult content but is small.
- **Dating apps** are not viable: face verification, terms violations, and catfishing or fraud exposure.

### Cited Findings

#### Reddit
- Sitewide policy bans nude or sexual imagery of a person posted without permission, "including depictions that have been faked." This dates from the 2018 update after the r/deepfakes ban — [TechCrunch, Feb 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/02/07/deepfakes-fake-porn-reddit-twitter-ban/?ncid=rss) (pre-2025; still the basis of the policy, but check the current wording).
- A Cornell Tech CHI 2025 study of 300k+ subreddits found that only 1.2% had AI rules (data from Nov 2024). That share has more than doubled since July 2023, and larger subreddits are much more likely to have AI rules — [arXiv 2410.11698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11698) [academic].
- Reddit uses its own AI tools against spam; users saw 20% less spam from Jan to Mar 2026 — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2208616/reddit-to-combat-ai-with-more-ai).
- Reddit added UK age controls in response to the OSA, per Ofcom's March 2026 review as reported — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/ofcom-telegram-investigation-csam-online-safety-act).
- Paid posts in high-traffic subreddits sell for $30–150 — [Sirency agency guide 2026](https://www.sirency.com/blog/influencer-marketing-onlyfans-agencies-2026) [vendor/anecdotal].

#### TikTok
- Requires labels on realistic AI images, audio and video. TikTok may apply an "AI-generated" label automatically, and creators can't remove it — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content) [primary].
- In Nov 2025 and through 2026, TikTok added tools to "spot, shape and understand" AI content and began teaching users that AI spam "crowds out original creators" — [TikTok Newsroom, 24 Nov 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-generated-content?lang=en-GB); [Tubefilter, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/14/tiktok-ai-transparency-tools-content-labels/); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/tech/tiktok-says-ai-generated-spam-crowds-out-original-creators-so-its-teaching-users-how-to-spot-ai). Its Aug 2025 guideline update touched AI content and LIVE — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/).
- "Zero tolerance for content which promotes off-platform sexual services" (2026 enforcement after the BBC investigation) — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/tiktok-bans-ai-impersonation-accounts-targeting-black-female-influencers/story). A LIVE whose main purpose is sending people off-platform loses For You feed eligibility, and some links lead to content not allowed on TikTok — [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features) [primary].
- TikTok's ad policy (updated June 2026) bans adult services, including AI nudify apps — [TikTok Ads policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-adult-content) [primary].

#### YouTube Shorts
- Creators must disclose realistic altered or synthetic content at upload. Clearly unrealistic or animated content is exempt — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) [primary].

## 4. KPIs and benchmarks

### Takeaway
Independent benchmarks barely exist. Platform data from OnlyFans filings show most money comes from **non-subscription purchases** (PPV, tips, messages), and earnings are highly concentrated. Vendor data put **first-month churn around 40–55%** and **average monthly rebill around 60–70%**. There are **no reliable public benchmarks** for follower-to-subscriber conversion or customer acquisition cost (CAC).

  - Sources: [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/onlyfans); [Tubefilter, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/26/onlyfans-million-dollar-earners-keily-blair-earnings/amp/) [secondary; figures conflict; filings not read].

- For comparison, the median voluntary churn across subscription businesses is 5.6%/month (Recurly 2024, cited second-hand), against 25–50% for OnlyFans pages — [CreatorGrowLabs](https://creatorgrowlabs.pages.dev/blog/benchmark-watch-churn-in-2026/) [anecdotal].

  - On X: $20–100 for 10K–50K followers, $100–400 for 50K–200K. Instagram stories: $50–200 for 50K–200K. Reddit posts: $30–150 — [Sirency 2026](https://www.sirency.com/blog/influencer-marketing-onlyfans-agencies-2026) [vendor].

- **Follower-to-subscriber conversion:** no numeric benchmark found; sources only name it as a metric to track — [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/marketing-on-onlyfans-how-to-market-onlyfans).

### Inferences
- **Lifetime value maths:** at 45–55% monthly churn, the average subscriber stays about 2 months, so LTV is roughly 2× monthly ARPU. The first 30 days of PPV and DM monetisation therefore decide most of the value. Rebill rate and month-1 churn are the KPIs to manage weekly.
- **Track everything as cohorts by source.** Use a separate tracking link per traffic source (IG bio, Threads, X, Telegram, each paid shoutout) and measure:
  - click → free follow → paid sub;
  - 30/60/90-day rebill;
  - ARPU split: subscription vs PPV vs tips vs voice/AI chat;
  - refunds and chargebacks.
- **Paid shoutouts:** test small, one placement at a time, and compute CAC per *rebilled* subscriber, not per signup.
- **Never buy followers or subscribers.** It adds no revenue, and in the US the FTC's fake social-media-indicator ban makes it an enforcement risk (Q5).

- No independent follower→paid conversion, CAC or LTV benchmarks for AI personas; Fanvue publishes no cohort or ARPU data.
- No data on the PPV/DM share of revenue for Fanvue AI creators specifically.
- Did not find Goldman Sachs' 2025–2026 creator-economy updates. Its 2023 forecast ($480B by 2027) was not re-verified and would be outdated.

## 5. Regulation as of late 2026: EU AI Act Art. 50 and Code of Practice, US FTC and state laws, UK OSA, deepfake laws

### Takeaway
- **EU:** Art. 50 transparency duties have applied since **2 Aug 2026**. The Digital Omnibus did not delay them, apart from a grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for providers' machine-readable marking. The final **Code of Practice (10 Jun 2026)** and **Commission Guidelines (20 Jul 2026)** imply that a photoreal, plausibly real synthetic persona counts as a **"deepfake."** That means visible labels at first exposure for EU audiences, and any AI chat must say it is AI.
- **US:** there is no general AI-labelling mandate, but four rules hit specific tactics:
  - the FTC endorsement and fake-indicator rules;
  - the **TAKE IT DOWN Act**, enforced since 19 May 2026;
  - **New York's synthetic-performer ad law**, in force since 9 Jun 2026;
  - **NY and California companion-chatbot laws**.
- **UK:** OSA age checks gate adult teasers for UK users. Since 6 Feb 2026 it has been a crime to create sexual deepfakes of real adults without consent.
- **Safest posture:** disclose AI everywhere, never resemble or use real people, and label ads.

### Cited Findings

#### EU AI Act Article 50 and the Code of Practice
- **Timing and the Digital Omnibus:**
  - Art. 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026.
  - The Omnibus (provisional deal 6 May 2026; Council confirmation 13 May) left Art. 50 on schedule, except a four-month grace period for **Art. 50(2) marking** for systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 (to 2 Dec 2026). The duty to disclose AI interaction stays on 2 Aug 2026 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) [law firm].
  - Some sources report Council final approval on 29 Jun 2026 and entry into force on 27 Jul 2026 — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [AI Act Blog](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026) [conflicting procedural detail; check the Official Journal].
- **New Art. 5 prohibition:** the Omnibus adds a ban on AI systems that generate or manipulate **non-consensual intimate images or CSAM**. It also covers systems where such output is a reasonably foreseeable, reproducible result and safeguards are missing. Transition runs to 2 Dec 2026 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/).
- **Final Code of Practice** on marking and labelling AI-generated content, published 10 Jun 2026. It is voluntary. Section 1 covers providers (Art. 50(2) marking); Section 2 covers deployers (Art. 50(4) labelling) — [European Commission press release IP/26/1328](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1328) [primary, title verified]; [Commission digital-strategy news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f7b2a1c6-c053-45e5-b5d6-83411c67b0d9).
- **Guidelines and signatories:** the Commission finalised its Art. 50 Guidelines on **20 Jul 2026**. About **190 organisations** had signed the Code by 31 Jul. Signatories have the clearest route to showing compliance; non-signatories must justify their own measures and can expect closer scrutiny — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content) [law firm].
- **What deployers must do under Art. 50(4), per the Code** — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content):
  - labels must be "immediately recognisable without user interaction," stay visible "for a sufficient duration," be embedded in the content where possible, and be noticeable at first exposure;
  - there are **three EU icons**: fully AI-generated, AI-modified, and a basic icon with an interactive second layer;
  - "Audible disclaimers are required where visual disclosure is not possible";
  - a provider's watermark "does not discharge the deployer's duty to label";
  - deployers need proportionate internal processes, AI-literacy measures and review mechanisms.
- **The deepfake test under the Guidelines** — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content):
  - it is objective: "intent to deceive not required and photorealism not decisive";
  - "the subject need only be plausibly real";
  - it catches "AI-generated or de-aged actors and digital replicas";
  - it leaves out "wholly fantastical content, or content no audience would take as genuine."
  - The statutory definition (Art. 3(60)) covers AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content "that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful" — [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) [primary; statutory text, not re-fetched this session].
- **Creative or artistic works:** the duty shrinks to disclosing that generation or manipulation exists, in a way that does not hamper display or enjoyment of the work — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content).
- **AI text on matters of public interest:** read broadly (politics, **public health**, consumer safety, finance…). It must be disclosed unless a competent person exercises real, documented editorial control. Fiction, product descriptions and private chatbot replies are excluded — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content).
- **Art. 50(1), chatbots** — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content):
  - providers must make sure people know they are talking to AI, unless that is obvious to a reasonably well-informed user (read narrowly);
  - AI agents "must disclose both their artificial nature as well as the person on whose behalf they are acting";
  - disclosure "buried in terms and conditions" does not count;
  - information must come at the latest at first interaction (Art. 50(5)).
- **Penalties:** fines up to the greater of €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover — [Paul, Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content).
- **Reach:** the Act covers providers and deployers outside the EU when the AI output is used in the Union (Art. 2(1)(c)). It excludes natural persons using AI for purely personal, non-professional activity (Art. 2(10)) — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) [primary; not re-fetched this session].

#### Other EU and national rules
- **France** (influencer law, Loi 2023-451, amended Nov 2024): in commercial influencer content, images or videos of faces or bodies produced by AI must carry **"image virtuelle"** ("image retouchée" if edited). The mention must be clear, legible and visible throughout the promotion. Reported penalties vary: up to €4,500 and 1 year for labelling failures; the top tier of 2 years and €300,000 applies to other breaches — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2023/france/promulgation-de-la-loi-visant-a-encadrer-l-influence-commerciale) (2023); [economie.gouv.fr](https://economie.gouv.fr/influenceurs-quels-sont-mes-devoirs) [primary].
- **EU Digital Fairness Act proposal (adopted 11 Nov 2026; still only a proposal)** — [Euronews, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/11/eu-targets-subscription-traps-addictive-design-and-hidden-fees-with-new-digital-rules); [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/review-eu-consumer-law_en):
  - first EU-level influencer rules: disclose commercial content, and don't exploit children's trust;
  - easier cancellation; reminders before free trials turn into paid subscriptions; auto-renewal must be a "conscious choice";
  - ban on dark patterns such as fake "almost sold out" claims;
  - clearer conversion rates for virtual currencies;
  - ban on unfair personalised ads or pricing that target minors or exploit vulnerabilities;
  - a separate CPC revision could allow fines up to 6% of global turnover, and non-EU firms would need an EU legal representative.
  - Existing EU consumer-law guidance already treats influencers as "natural persons or virtual entities" — [Osborne Clarke, Sep 2025](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/digital-fairness-act-unpacked-social-media-influencers).

#### US federal
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (2023):** an "endorser" includes anyone who "could be or appear to be" an individual, which covers virtual influencers. Material connections must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, and influencers and intermediaries can be liable — [Federal Register, 26 Jul 2023](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising) [primary]; [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-am/insights/publications/2023/07/federal-trade-commission-announces-changes-to-the-endorsement-guidelines) (2023, still the current guidance).
- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** (final 14 Aug 2024; effective 21 Oct 2024): bans fake reviews and the **sale or purchase of fake indicators of social-media influence** (bot followers, views, etc.) for commercial purposes. Civil penalties apply to knowing violations ($51,744 per violation in 2024, adjusted for inflation each year) — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) [primary]; [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials) [primary].
- **TAKE IT DOWN Act:** the FTC has enforced the notice-and-removal duty since **19 May 2026**.
  - Covered platforms must remove non-consensual intimate imagery, including AI "digital forgeries" of identifiable people, plus known identical copies, **within 48 hours** of a valid request.
  - Violations count as FTC-rule violations, at $53,088 per violation.
  - The FTC opened a complaint portal (TakeItDown.ftc.gov) and sent warning letters to a dozen companies on 20 May 2026. Criminal arrests have also been announced.
  - Sources: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act) [primary]; [FTC business guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act) [primary]; [FTC warning letters](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-sends-warning-letters-companies-about-compliance-take-it-down-act); [Orrick, Jun 2026](https://www.orrick.com/fr-FR/Insights/2026/06/Nonconsensual-Intimate-Images-Online-Take-It-Down-Act-Enforcement-In-Full-Swing).
- **Executive order of 11 Dec 2025:**
  - creates an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws (from about 10 Jan 2026), plus a Commerce Department review and leverage over BEAD broadband funds;
  - a federal framework is to follow, with carve-outs including child safety;
  - the order **does not itself displace state laws**.
  - Sources: [White House fact sheet](https://whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/12/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-ensures-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence) [primary]; [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/new-executive-order-aims-to-preempt-state-ai-regulation); [Ropes & Gray, Mar 2026](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/examining-the-landscape-and-limitations-of-the-federal-push-to-override-state-ai-regulation).

#### US state laws
- **New York synthetic-performer ad disclosure** (GBL §396-b; signed 11 Dec 2025; in force **9 Jun 2026**):
  - advertisers with actual knowledge must conspicuously disclose synthetic performers in ads shown to NY audiences, wherever the advertiser is based;
  - the form of disclosure is not specified;
  - penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each later one.
  - Sources: [Cooley, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102n129/fake-performer-real-penalty-what-advertisers-need-to-know-before-june-9/); [News12, 10 Jun 2026](https://brooklyn.news12.com/2026/06/10/ads-in-new-york-must-now-label-ai-generated-synthetic-performers/KAoANpKS5shi4rKqcypqF).
- **AI companion laws** — [Morrison Foerster, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-the-new-wave-of-chatbot-legislation-california-sb-243-and-beyond/) [law firm/policy]:
  - *New York* (GBL Art. 47, in force 5 Nov 2025): disclose that it is AI at the start of each interaction and at least every 3 hours; protocols for suicidal ideation and self-harm; the attorney general can seek up to $15,000/day.
  - *California SB 243* (in force 1 Jan 2026): disclose where a reasonable person could be misled into thinking they are talking to a human; 3-hour reminders for known minors; crisis protocols; annual reports from 1 Jul 2027; private right of action for the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation.

### Inferences: what this operator should do
1. **EU labelling.**

6. **UK reach.** Adult teasers on X and Telegram will be age-gated for UK users. Keep avatars and headers SFW, per X's rules.

## 6. Payment processors and app stores

### Takeaway
Card networks rarely act in public. Pressure flows through acquirers and processors under adult-merchant rules and brand-protection clauses: Mastercard's 2021 standards and Rule 5.12.7. Civitai (2025) and Steam/itch.io (2025) show the pattern. AI sexual content tied to **real people** is the clearest red line; fully synthetic adult content is tolerated on Fanvue. Because OnlyFans and Fansly don't accept AI personas built from scratch, **Fanvue is a single point of failure.**

### Cited Findings
- **Mastercard's 2021 adult-content standards:**
  - documented age and identity verification, and consent, for everyone depicted and for the uploader;
  - content review before publication;
  - a complaints process that resolves reports of illegal or non-consensual content within 7 business days;
  - an appeals route for people who are depicted.
  - Sources: [Mastercard, Apr 2021](https://mastercard.com/news/perspectives/2021/protecting-our-network-protecting-you-preventing-illegal-adult-content-on-our-network) [primary]; [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-04-14/mastercard-rewrites-rules-for-banks-backing-pornography-sellers) (pre-2025; no later update found).
- **Steam and itch.io, Jul–Aug 2025:** both removed or delisted adult games.
  - Mastercard said on 1 Aug 2025 that it had not evaluated any games or required restrictions, and only requires merchants to block unlawful purchases.
  - Valve said its processors and acquiring banks cited **Mastercard Rule 5.12.7** and brand risk.
  - Stripe said it is "unable to support sexually explicit content" because of banking partners.
  - Sources: [TechCrunch, 3 Aug 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/03/mastercard-denies-pressuring-game-platforms-valve-tells-a-different-story); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/mastercard-deflects-blame-for-nsfw-games-being-taken-down-but-valve-says-payment-processors-specifically-cited-a-mastercard-rule-about-damaging-the-brand).

## 7. Reputation and ethics best practices for AI-model operators

### Takeaway
Regulation, platform rules and the 2024–2026 scandals point to the same practices:
- full transparency about AI status, and about who or what is chatting;
- no use of real people's faces, bodies or likeness;
- content that is unambiguously adult in age;
- honest commercial disclosures;
- no manipulative monetisation;
- respect for human creators.

### Cited Findings
- **Partnership on AI, Responsible Practices for Synthetic Media** (2023, living document; voluntary, no certification). For creators it recommends transparency, disclosing synthetic elements, informed consent of subjects, and repairing harm. A 2024 study applying it to 11 real cases found emerging best practices around consent, disclosure, and separating harmful from creative uses — [PAI framework](https://partnershiponai.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/PAI_synthetic_media_framework.pdf) [primary]; [arXiv 2407.13025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13025); [MacArthur Foundation summary](https://www.macfound.org/press/grantee-publications/framework-for-ethical-synthetic-media) (pre-2025, still referenced).
- **Mislabelling is common:** 60% of creators admit AI-assisted content broke industry rules, often through mislabelling — [eMarketer/Billion Dollar Boy](https://www.emarketer.com/content/exclusive--ai-slop-threat-creator-economy).
- **Face swaps onto real women's bodies:** these drove the 2024 "AI pimping" coverage and Meta's account deletions. Fanvue says using images that steal someone's identity breaks its rules, uses a third-party moderation tool plus human moderators, and called deepfakes "an industry challenge" — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ai-influencers-are-deepfaking-their-faces-onto-real-womens-bodies/); [BI via AOL](https://www.aol.com/influencers-using-ai-women-lead-093001152.html).
- **Fake intimacy in DMs** produced class actions against OnlyFans; one case goes to trial in 2027 — [ClassAction.org](https://classaction.org/news/onlyfans-lawsuit-alleges-subscribers-unknowingly-talk-with-paid-chatters-not-actual-content-creators).
- **Who discloses matters:** being exposed by a third party hurts trust more than disclosing yourself — [University of Arizona](https://news.arizona.edu/news/disclosing-ai-use-can-backfire-research-shows).
- **Platforms now enforce disclosure:** Instagram (reach limits), TikTok and YouTube (labels and demonetisation) — [Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label); [TikTok](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en).
- **Societal criticism:** AI influencers are said to reinforce unrealistic beauty standards — [NationalWorld](https://www.nationalworld.com/culture/celebrity/ai-influencers-mia-zelu-alba-renai-5222935).

### Inferences: best-practice code for this operator
1. **Disclose at every layer:** the Instagram "AI-generated profile" toggle; "AI-generated" or "virtual creator" in every bio; the EU icon or AI tag on media for EU reach; Fanvue's AI badge. If anyone asks whether she is real, never deny being AI.
2. **Be honest about who is talking.** Label AI Messaging and AI Voice Notes as AI. If humans moderate or write messages, say so in the profile. Never stage "live" calls with a human pretending to be the persona.
3. **No real-person inputs:** no face swaps, no training on identifiable people without written consent, no use of real creators' videos for motion, no celebrity resemblance. Run periodic lookalike checks and keep provenance records of the base model, LoRA training data and prompts.
4. **Age clarity:** keep the persona's 40-something presentation consistent. Avoid any youthful framing ("girl," school or teen cues), in line with Fanvue's minor-resemblance rule.
5. **Honest commerce:**
   - Mark sponsorships as ads (FTC, EU, France's "image virtuelle").
   - Don't sell fitness programmes or supplements using AI-generated body "results" as proof.
   - Avoid health claims; YouTube demonetises AI personas on medical topics, and EU public-interest text rules may apply.
6. **No manipulative monetisation:** no fake scarcity, no pressure loops in PPV, easy cancellation, no targeting of signs of vulnerability. Have an escalation script for fans who show distress, in the spirit of the NY/CA companion laws. Never buy fake followers or engagement.
7. **Respect human creators:** don't repost others' content. When approaching SFS partners, say upfront that the account is AI.
8. **Prepare for incidents:** keep a takedown and response playbook (lookalike claim, leak, deepfake accusation). Keep receipts of the generation provenance. Plan for the possibility that "exposure" stories target the category even when the persona is compliant.
